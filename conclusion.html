Section 04

Conclusion

What our project found, what it contributes, and what questions it raises for the future.

Does Our Project Answer the Research Question?

Our research question asked: does NFL team performance correlate with Instagram follower growth? The honest answer is yes, the two do correlate for most teams but there are a couple of outliers. An example of this is seen with the Miami Dolphins. In 2024, they only had 8 wins, yet the team still hit 4M followers on Instagram. This was when Alix Earle (a famous TikTok influencer) started dating Braxton Berrios (Miami’s punt returner). This connects with the celebrity culture and sports crossover research that we did, showing that this can have an impact on social media regardless of their performance on the field.

An example of a team’s performance making a statistical impact on their social media account is seen with the Cincinnati Bengals. In 2021 they were at 2M followers. In 2022 they had a 12-win season where Joe Burrow led the Bengals to a Super Bowl. This directly spiked their follower count to 3.5M. This 1.5M follower account boost proves that our hypothesis is correct, and that most of the time performance does correlate with follower engagement.

We found evidence of a clear, positive relationship between winning and follower growth. When teams like the Detroit Lions or Philadelphia Eagles dramatically improved their on-field performance and their record, their Instagram followings grew noticeably faster along with that. This made it clear that performance does matter.

But on the other hand the data also shows clear limits to our question. Established and known franchises like the Dallas Cowboys grow their audiences steadily without as much regard for whether they win or lose. And in several instances like with the Kansas City Chiefs, off-field cultural moments caused follower spikes that no level of on-field performance could plausibly do on its own (thank you Taylor Swift).

Our interactive visualizations also make this data accessible and explorable in ways that a static paper simply cannot. Readers can zoom in on specific seasons and see exactly where cultural events align with follower trends for given teams. We think letting the data tell its own story rather than relying solely on our interpretation is important in this specific project, and we encourage you to check it out and play with it on your own.

New Questions Raised

Questions for Future Research
  • →How does this pattern differ across other social platforms? Does TikTok, Twitter, etc. show stronger or weaker correlations with performance compared to Instagram?
  • →How do individual player accounts factor into all of this? If a star player leaves a team, does the team’s follower count suffer? If so, by how much?
Final Reflection 🎉
That’s a Wrap!
What surprised us most in this project was not that cultural moments matter (we suspected that going in), but how much they matter in relation to winning. The NFL is the most-watched sports league in the United States, and its teams compete not only for wins but also for media attention. In that environment, attaching a franchise to a cultural hit like Taylor Swift or Simone Biles can be worth more Instagram followers than a Super Bowl run.

Social media following is not a perfect measure of fan loyalty or team quality, but it can be utilized as a little glimpse into how sports brands intersect with popular culture.
