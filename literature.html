Section 01

Literature
Review

A survey of research on social media, sports fandom, and the relationship between athletic performance and digital audience growth.

Social Media & Sports Organizations

NFL teams have really started to lean into Instagram as a way to connect with their fans in a more real, everyday way. When you actually look at what works, it’s not necessarily the super polished, overly promotional posts. Instead, it is often the behind-the-scenes moments, the locker room clips, the player personalities, something unique, or the little things fans wouldn’t normally get to see. That kind of content just feels more genuine, and people respond to it way more. At the end of the day, it shows that fans ultimately care about feeling connected even more than winning or stats, at least when it comes to social media. Personality and access matter just as much as performance, if not more.

Athletic Performance & Fan Engagement

Several studies have examined this direct relationship between on-field success and fan behavior that we speak of. It has been found that winning percentage is positively connected to game attendance, merchandise sales, and overall media exposure. However, their findings also showed that this effect has variables. For example, teams that are consistently good do not see massive increases in engagement over time.

Celebrity Culture & Sports Crossover

An important aspect of sports social media growth that has not been talked about enough is the role of celebrity culture and media crossover. We see lots of correlation between the popularity of outside celebrities (not football players) and the engagement levels of sports teams’ social media accounts if fans find them in a relationship or a fan of a team.

Instagram as a Research Subject

Instagram launched in 2010 and was acquired by Facebook in 2012. It has since become a main platform for sports content. Visual platforms such as this are largely well-suited to sports content because they capture the aesthetic and emotional aspects of athletic performance in ways that a text-only method simply cannot.
